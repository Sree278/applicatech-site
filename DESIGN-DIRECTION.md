# Aplica / Advanced Concept 05
## Business understanding. Connected delivery.
**Local design prototype; not approved production content or validated customer UX.**

## The approved upgrade
The cinematic exploration is extended into a more useful evaluation journey: an authored 3D structure, a business-scenario explorer, a complete representative implementation page and an evidence-led customer-story template.

## Design decisions
### 1. A structural visual identity
An original layered, twisting framework replaces the floating-cube study. Translucent planes add depth, while a continuous warm route links the tiers. It suggests relationships and coordination, not an actual customer architecture. It uses custom WebGL, not a downloaded 3D model, video or external runtime library.

### 2. A visual response with a textual explanation
Four scenarios: finance/reporting; operations/inventory; rental/project workflows; existing Dynamics systems. A selection changes the structure's emphasis and colour alongside the HTML explanation. The selected state is also identified by labels, border and ARIA state: colour is not the only signal.

Each scenario describes relationships, a useful question and a scope limitation. No automated diagnosis, product-fit result, ROI or promised customer outcome is generated.

### 3. Depth beyond the homepage
The implementation page explains the buying situation, scope questions, possible engagement outputs, relevant evidence and FAQs. It is a representative content journey, not a signed-off service contract. The page makes it possible to evaluate whether the design can carry substantive content.

### 4. Evidence before results
The case-study page is explicitly an editorial template. It shows how to document context, decisions, delivery, evidence and permissions. There is no fictional customer, disguised testimonial, invented percentage or unsupported project timeline. Approved real evidence remains necessary.

### 5. Motion hierarchy
- Hero: short, naturally scrolled transformation; no wheel interception.
- Explorer: selection-driven visual response and a restrained text-panel translation.
- Service rows: expand with real content and clear links.
- Page transitions: optional cross-document browser enhancement; standard navigation remains the fallback.
- Mobile: a simpler opening, stacked explorer and linear detail content.
- Reduced motion: static scene and no ornamental/page transitions.

## Brand and colour roles
- Ink #080E18: the primary environment.
- Icy white #E9EFF4: important content.
- Electric blue #306BFF: technical visual emphasis, not small text on ink.
- Apricot #E4B08A: direction and selected cues.
- Pale surface #E6EBEC: evaluation and evidence sections.
- Finance blue, operational teal, rental amber and existing-system violet are illustrative scenario accents. They are not regulatory/status indicators or product colours.

Typography: Inter Tight for direct hierarchy, Instrument Serif italic for selective expressive contrast, IBM Plex Mono for supporting labels. Fonts are self-hosted. The typographic wordmark is exploratory, not a completed rebrand.

## Resilience and performance decisions
- Reduced geometry and pixel-ratio cap for lower reported hardware capacity; capped rendering cadence (approximately 20 fps on the lower-capacity branch, 30 fps otherwise).
- Rendering stops when the relevant visual is offscreen or the document is hidden.
- Ambient motion control applies to both scenes. User-driven selection may still update the static scene.
- WebGL failure shows a CSS illustration; all business information stays in HTML.
- Canvas resizing follows its container, including changing content height.
- Device capacity signals are coarse hints, not an accurate benchmark. Real-device performance/battery testing is still required.

## Information architecture
Home → implementation approach → customer-story format → enquiry.
The homepage also supports improvement and support discussions. Other full service pages are not included in this prototype. The contact form validates and prepares an email draft; there is no backend, CRM or fake submission.

## What remains before production
Commercial/service verification, approved customer cases and asset rights, authentic team/product imagery, customer task research, broader accessibility and real-device testing, a production CMS implementation, legal/privacy content, secured enquiry processing where needed, analytics and SEO migration planning.

No award, conversion uplift, comprehensive accessibility conformance or validated competitive advantage is claimed. The work targets stronger craft and clearer evaluation—not spectacle at the expense of comprehension.

## Visual art-direction refinement
Two original AI-generated conceptual artworks have been added:
- A blue glass / brushed-metal ribbon with an apricot route: a visual metaphor for continuity, used in the opening and implementation page.
- Intersecting blue glass and cobalt forms on pale stone: a visual metaphor for perspectives and evidence, used in the evidence section and story template.

They are decorative/conceptual—not live 3D objects, actual product interfaces, customer photographs or proof of a completed project. The original WebGL study remains a separate interactive layer and powers the scenario explorer. Figures are labelled accordingly.

Compositional changes: split editorial heroes, image-led evidence section, original service-line SVG motifs, softer framing, restrained material lighting, more deliberate section spacing and a quieter sidebar hierarchy. Dark and pale surfaces retain role-specific text colours. Artwork uses optimised WebP copies (approximately 72 KB and 82 KB) rather than the full PNG sources at runtime. Alt text/captions are included where images convey a concept; the decorative homepage image is hidden from assistive technology.

The purpose is stronger visual identity and rhythm, not a claim that more imagery improves conversion. Real people, customer proof and user research remain outstanding.
