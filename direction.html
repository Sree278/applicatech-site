Practical expertise. Clear delivery.

Proposed design direction, branding system and decision register
31 October 2026 · Prepared for discussion, not a client-approved rebrand.

1. The brief

Create an identifiable, approachable Microsoft business-applications consultancy website that helps a prospective buyer understand where Aplica can help and how to start. Respond to the client meeting's Middle East priority, interest in blue branding, storytelling preference, maintainability needs and budget constraints.

Inputs: client meeting; desk research in Aplica-Research-and-Hypotheses.md; existing published capabilities and testimonial excerpts. No customer interviews, sales analysis, analytics or validated audience research were available. The discovery is hypothesis-led. This concept is a recommendation, not proof of commercial impact.

Production assumptions: English first; WordPress remains the preferred future production CMS; no Arabic, domain migration, full rebrand rollout or CRM integration implied. This static HTML/CSS/JS prototype tests direction and structure only.

2. The strategic direction

Business clarity + technical specificity + human collaboration.

Working positioning: “Practical Microsoft Dynamics expertise for businesses implementing, improving or supporting their systems.”

Working expression: “Practical expertise. Clear delivery.”

Headline exploration: “Complex business. Practical answers.”

These are proposed copy, not approved claims of superior service. A direct Microsoft business-applications label accompanies the expressive headline so a visitor need not infer the category.

Why this territory?

The research found broad connected-systems benefits repeated across the category. Aplica's public offer contains implementation, recovery, upgrades and ongoing support; customer excerpts describe collaboration and technical delivery. This suggests practical delivery as a territory to validate. It does not establish exclusivity or an objectively better service.

Intended audience situations

  1. Planning a new implementation.
  2. Improving an existing Dynamics environment.
  3. Seeking support and continued improvement.

These are candidate buying situations, not validated market segments. Industry, project size, decision roles and country priorities need confirmation.

3. Brand character

  • Capable, without inflated claims.
  • Clear, without stripping out technical substance.
  • Human, without informal or consumer-style language.
  • Distinctive, without unnecessary visual spectacle.
  • Practical, without looking low-cost or generic.

Not: an AI-first startup, a product marketplace, a luxury agency, a Microsoft look-alike or a reseller brochure.

5. Imagery strategy

This is not a no-images direction. It is a purposeful evidence-led imagery strategy.

Used in the prototype

  • Original SVG architectural diagram: three layers and one copper route represent understanding, delivery and improvement. Explicitly conceptual, not a product screen or actual system architecture.
  • Original SVG collaboration illustration: a temporary editorial stand-in for a team/workshop photograph. The image is labelled; it does not depict actual staff.
  • Structured workflow graphics: illustrative discussion scenarios, not customer results.

Required for production

  1. Authentic team or workshop photograph: replace the collaboration illustration, with consent and useful crop options.
  2. Approved project imagery: support a relevant case study where available.
  3. Current, permitted product screenshots: explain capabilities on platform pages; label custom/demo interfaces correctly.
  4. Approved customer logos and reference quotations: confirm permission, context and attribution.

No generic stock boardroom photographs, fabricated customer portraits, AI-generated images passed off as actual people, or screenshots implying unverified capabilities. A photo is valuable when it explains who does the work or substantiates the story—not because every section must contain one.

6. Information architecture decisions

Main concept navigation: Expertise / Experience / Our approach / About / Let's talk.

The service overview is a real second HTML page, with implementation, improvement and support sections. This checks that the visual system can carry deeper content, not only a homepage.

Homepage sequence: 1. Proposition + Microsoft category context. 2. Platform orientation. 3. Three buying-situation routes. 4. Business-context scenarios. 5. Customer evidence framework. 6. Proposed delivery narrative. 7. People/company story. 8. FAQs. 9. Contact.

Key trade-off: production should bring approved customer proof closer to the opening. In this concept there is no permission-cleared case study, so the early experience section uses explicitly labelled scenarios. The evidence framework is an internal-review device, not a substitute for a published case. Replace or omit it before launch.

Do not conclude that this sequence converts better; test it. Final dedicated service, platform and industry pages depend on commercial priority, publishable evidence and SEO migration planning. Do not discard existing URLs without an audit and redirect map.

7. Content decisions

  • Describe implementation, improvement and support as entry routes because the published offer is broader than new ERP sales alone.
  • Keep Microsoft products visible, but do not turn the homepage into a feature catalogue.
  • Use questions that connect to workflows; mark them as examples, not anonymised customer success stories.
  • Use no invented metrics, customer quotations, credentials, headcount, partner tiers or fixed implementation times.
  • Do not assert blanket GCC payroll or regulatory compliance. Country/product requirements need technical verification.
  • Mention the existing source website for public customer material; its publication does not constitute permission to repurpose every asset.
  • Use “proposed”, “illustrative” and scope notes where necessary. Remove editorial review scaffolding only after content approval, not merely to make the page look finished.

8. Interaction decisions

  • Service links navigate to a real service page.
  • Business scenario tabs work with click, arrow keys, Home and End; selection and panels use ARIA relationships.
  • FAQs use native details/summary.
  • Mobile navigation supports Escape and resets after selection.
  • Native form validation precedes mailto draft creation. This is not server submission; the page explicitly states nothing is sent or stored by the site.
  • Contact links and CTA labels describe the actual action. If no email client exists, the status gives a direct-address fallback.
  • Visible focus styles, semantic landmarks, one h1 per page, self-hosted fonts and responsive breakpoints are included. This is not an accessibility certification.
  • No analytics, cookies, third-party runtime assets or tracking scripts are included. External links take the visitor off-site. GitHub Pages infrastructure can still process normal request metadata.

9. What changed from Concepts 01 and 02

01: dark editorial Authored technical imagery, hierarchy and a distinctive identity Reduce density; remove unapproved delivery/metric narratives; keep dark surfaces selective.
02: light blue Readability, breathing room and straightforward language Stronger typographic identity, warmer surfaces, more relevant service routes, real service-page depth.

Concept 03 is not “research-proven.” It is a more traceable hypothesis: every major choice has a reason, a trade-off and a validation step.

10. Approval gates

Before expanding design: - Leadership: confirm priority buyer situation, target countries and commercial offerings. - Sales: validate the entry routes and actual reasons customers choose Aplica. - Delivery: verify capability scope, scenarios and realistic process language. - Brand owner: approve or reject blue/copper, type pairing and proposed mark. - Customer owners: approve two stories, quotations, logos and use permissions.

Before production: - Confirm WordPress editor approach, maintenance ownership and migration scope. - Replace review placeholders; approve legal and privacy content. - Implement secured forms, spam controls and agreed data handling if required. - Test browser/device layouts, keyboard/screen-reader behaviour, contrast and performance. - Validate routes, redirects, metadata, analytics plan and launch checklist. - Establish baseline enquiry quality and tracking; do not promise conversion uplift.

11. Validation questions

  1. After a short look, can a relevant buyer explain what Aplica does?
  2. Can they find their starting route without knowing a product name?
  3. Can an existing Dynamics customer see that improvement/support may be relevant?
  4. Can they locate credible evidence—and distinguish it from scenarios?
  5. Does the visual identity feel recognisable and appropriate to Aplica, rather than merely attractive?
  6. Is the next step clear and honest?

Client explanation

“We recommend a practical consultancy identity: clear enough to understand quickly, technically specific enough to evaluate, and human enough to approach. The blue-led system responds to the brand discussion, while warm surfaces, selective serif typography and original diagrams give it character. The structure reflects three reasons a customer may need help: implementing, improving or supporting their systems. We will substantiate that direction with approved customer evidence before treating it as a production website.”
