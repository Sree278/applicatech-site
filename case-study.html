CONNECTED DELIVERY.

01 / BEGIN WITH CONTEXT

What needed
to change?

A useful story starts with a specific customer situation, not a list of product features. Explain the operational problem, constraints and reason for acting.

CONTENT REQUIRED

An approved customer narrative

Customer identity or agreed anonymisation, industry, relevant geography, existing landscape and the business trigger. None has been supplied for this template.

02 / SHOW THE THINKING

Why this approach?
What were the trade-offs?

Describe the alternatives considered, scope boundaries, product-fit decisions and responsibilities. Separate Aplica's work from the customer's and other partners' contributions.

Starting point→Decisions→Implemented scope

A narrative diagram—not a claimed architecture or a record of a delivered project.

03 / MAKE THE WORK SPECIFIC

What actually
happened?

Explain the delivery with an approved process diagram, permitted product screenshots or project imagery. Identify material challenges and what changed as the team learned.

  • What was implemented, integrated or improved?
  • How were data, users and operational continuity considered?
  • What was outside the scope?
  • Which decisions were specific to this customer?
VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

Real evidence, not a decorative dashboard.

Use an approved diagram or screenshot with an accurate caption. Confirm confidentiality, asset rights and any needed redaction before publication.

04 / SUBSTANTIATE THE OUTCOME

What changed?
What can be verified?

Evidence fields to complete—not published performance metrics
Evidence fieldRequired before publishing
Starting pointSource and date for any baseline; explain the measure.
Observed changeCustomer-confirmed qualitative or quantitative result, with relevant period.
AttributionWhat the work contributed, without claiming sole causation where unsupported.
QuotationExact approved wording, speaker identity, role and permission.
LimitsRelevant conditions, scope and uncertainties.

No placeholder percentages, fictional names, invented quotations or unsupported timelines are used.

05 / APPROVAL BEFORE PUBLICATION

Build confidence.
Do not simulate it.

  1. Interview the customer and delivery lead.
  2. Check project scope and source evidence.
  3. Approve names, quotations, visuals and results.
  4. Obtain publication permission and complete confidentiality review.
  5. Confirm accuracy, context, date and an owner for future updates.
